Children usually lie to avoid disappointing you or getting into trouble. That they know they did something wrong shows their conscience is developing. Calm responses and making the truth feel safe help more than harsh punishment.

## Background: When to get extra help

If lying has become a regular, ongoing pattern, talk to your pediatrician or a counselor, child-guidance clinic or mental health professional for support.

Sources: aap-lying

## Win: Make telling the truth the easier choice

### Action
When you catch a lie, calmly let your child know straight away that you know it isn't true. Skip harsh punishment, which tends to backfire. Explain that telling the truth means less trouble, and model honesty yourself, including avoiding little "white lies" in front of them.

### Say this
"I don't think that's what happened. I want us to always tell each other the truth. You'll be in much less trouble if you tell me the truth."

### What to expect
Your child may still deny it at first. Staying calm rather than angry makes it feel safer to own up next time. If you overreact, children can feel they need to keep lying to protect themselves, so steady and calm works better over time.
