Potty training goes most smoothly when your child is ready and the potty becomes an ordinary part of the day. Calm, steady practice and lots of encouragement work better than pressure.

## Background: Signs your child may be ready

Children may be ready when they stay dry for about 2 hours during the day or after naps, show they're about to pee or poop (grunting, freezing or squatting), can follow simple instructions, can walk to the bathroom and help undress, dislike wet diapers, or ask to use the potty or wear "big-kid" underwear.

Sources: aap-potty-age

## Win: Make the potty part of your daily routine

### Action
Don't wait for your child to tell you they need to go. Offer the potty at the same moments every day: when they get up, after eating, before leaving the house, before naps and before bed. Use a child-sized potty where their feet can reach the floor.

### Say this
"We just finished breakfast, so it's potty time. Let's sit for a little while and read a book."

### What to expect
Your child won't always go, and that's fine: sitting is the practice. Praise the effort either way. Accidents are a normal part of learning, so try not to get upset: stay calm, clean up together and move on.
