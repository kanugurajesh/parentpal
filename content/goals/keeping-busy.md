Good play doesn't need expensive toys. Everyday things you already have at home can keep a young child happily exploring, drawing and building, and your child learns most when you join in for a while first.

## Win: Make a box of everyday things to play with

### Action
Fill a box with clean, safe household items: cardboard boxes, yogurt pots, plastic bottles, old envelopes, scarves or an old hat. Make sure nothing is small enough to swallow. Sit with your child for the first few minutes to show one idea, like making a puppet from a sock or drawing on the inside of a cereal box, then let them carry on.

### Say this
"Here's our making box. Let's see what we can build. You start, and I'll check back in a minute."

### What to expect
Children often enjoy simple, familiar things as much as shop-bought toys. At first your child may want you to stay and play, which is a good sign. Join in, then step back a little more each time. Swapping a few items every so often keeps the box interesting.
