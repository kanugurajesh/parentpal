Bedwetting is common and often runs in families. It can be upsetting, but most children grow out of it. It's not your child's fault, and calm, practical steps help more than pressure.

## Background: When to see a doctor

See your doctor if bedwetting hasn't improved with the steps at home, or if your child starts wetting the bed again after being dry for 6 months or more. A doctor can talk you through options such as a bedwetting alarm or medicine.

Sources: nhs-bedwetting

## Win: Set up evenings for drier nights

### Action
Make sure your child drinks enough during the day, and avoid drinks with caffeine like cola, tea or coffee. Have them use the toilet regularly through the day and just before bed. Make the toilet easy to reach at night (a night light and a clear path), and use a waterproof mattress cover.

### Say this
"Last stop before bed is the toilet. If you need to go in the night, the light is on and the way is clear."

### What to expect
Change is usually gradual. Praise the steps your child can control, like using the toilet before bed, rather than whether they woke up dry. Never punish wet nights: it isn't their fault and can make bedwetting worse.
