About 1 in 5 children learn to talk or use words later than other children their age. Lots of talking, reading and playing together gives your child the words and practice they need, and if you're worried, getting checked early makes a real difference.

## Background: When to talk to your doctor

If you're concerned about your child's speech or language, talk to your pediatrician: the sooner the better, because early help can make a significant difference. Speak up straight away if your child stops using words they used to say. If you're told to wait but are still worried, it's fine to ask for a second opinion or a referral to a speech-language therapist.

Sources: aap-language-delay

## Win: Talk through your day together

### Action
Name and describe what you're doing and seeing throughout the day: getting dressed, cooking, walking to the shop. Encourage your child to "talk" back with sounds, gestures or words, and respond to every attempt. Add a few minutes of play each day where your child leads, and read together.

### Say this
"We're washing the apples. One red apple, two red apples. Your turn: what color is this one?"

### What to expect
The more words your child hears in real conversations, the more they have to learn from. Progress builds over weeks and months. Keep it playful rather than testing. This doesn't replace getting your child checked if you're worried.
