Some children regularly resist going to school. They may get headaches, stomachaches, nausea or dizziness that show up mostly on school days and disappear at weekends. These feelings are real, and a calm, understanding but firm approach helps them get back on track.

## Background: When to get help

Ask your pediatrician to check for any illness and help plan your child's return to school. If your child is still avoiding school after about a week, a mental health professional can help with the worries underneath.

Sources: aap-school-avoidance

## Win: Listen to the worry, then keep going to school

### Action
At a calm time, ask your child why they don't want to go, and listen with sympathy. Let them know you understand, and still keep them going to school. Talk with their teacher, the school nurse or principal so everyone can support them in the same way.

### Say this
"It sounds like school feels really hard right now, and I'm glad you told me. You're still going today, and your teacher and I are going to help."

### What to expect
Mornings may be tough for a while. Listening to the worry while keeping the routine shows your child that you take their feelings seriously and believe they can cope. Staying home tends to make going back harder.
