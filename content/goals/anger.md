Everybody gets mad. Anger isn't the problem; what children do with it is. They can learn to notice the feeling, calm their body and then sort out the problem with words, and they learn most by watching how the adults around them handle their own anger.

## Win: Practice a calm-down plan together

### Action
At a calm moment, teach a simple plan: notice the angry feeling in your body (hot face, tight fists), then calm down by breathing deeply, counting to 10 or walking away, and then talk about the problem. Practice it together when nobody is upset, and use it yourself out loud when you feel frustrated.

### Say this
"I can see you're really mad. Let's take three big breaths together, then you can tell me what happened."

### What to expect
In the heat of the moment your child may not manage it at first, and that's normal. Practicing when everyone is calm makes it easier to reach for later. Children copy what they see, so your own calm reactions are one of the strongest lessons.
