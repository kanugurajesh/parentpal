Separation anxiety is a normal part of development and usually eases by the preschool years. Short, predictable goodbyes and always coming back when you said you would help your child build trust and confidence.

## Win: Use a quick, same-every-time goodbye

### Action
Make up a short goodbye ritual, like a special handshake or three kisses, and use it every time, ideally at the same time each day. Give your child your full, warm attention, say goodbye, and leave even if they protest. Tell them when you'll be back in terms they understand, and always come back when you said.

### Say this
"Three kisses, a big hug, and I'm off. I'll be back after nap time. See you soon!"

### What to expect
Some tears at first are normal. If you linger, the hard part lasts longer, so keep it short and kind. Don't pop back early to check in, as that can restart the upset. Over time, keeping your promise teaches your child they can trust you'll return.
